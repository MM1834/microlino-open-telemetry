# JNY-001 — Journey Summary and Energy Email Pilot

> **Status:** Active — telemetry email physically validated; N16 firmware counter implemented for controlled REV11 pilot
>
> **Date:** 2026-08-14

## Objective

Add an optional, per-user and per-vehicle email after a qualifying completed
journey. The primary result is consumed net battery energy in kWh, accompanied by
distance, duration, SOC change, gross consumption, regeneration and net
kWh/100 km where the underlying evidence supports those values.

## Product boundary

Journey email is a separate opt-in from charging-target notification. It reuses
the existing confirmed notification email address and SNS subscription rather
than silently using the Cognito account address. It is informational only.

No route or GPS history is included. Pilot delivery may be exercised by an
explicitly opted-in user, but the capability is not called validated until the
journey-end detector, energy semantics and idempotent delivery path have passed
a controlled physical journey.

## Platform priority and firmware gate

The nanoESP32-C6-N16 is the priority-1 firmware target. Before firmware energy
integration is accepted, measure exact application flash, static RAM and runtime
heap impact on the canonical N16 build and confirm that CAN, GPS, WiFi, AWS,
WebUI and OTA margins do not regress.

The 4 MB XIAO is a non-blocking compatibility target. Compile and retain the
feature there only if it remains within the existing OTA-slot and runtime-memory
gates without a board-specific fork. Failure to fit XIAO does not block the N16
pilot.

No firmware change is authorized by the initial portal slice.

## Journey eligibility

Initial fixed pilot criteria are:

- at least 3 km;
- at least one consumed SOC percentage point;
- no confirmed charging followed by further odometer movement inside the same journey;
- monotonically increasing odometer;
- plausible distance/SOC and energy values;
- a stable end condition, initially ten minutes without resumed movement;
- exactly one summary event per user, vehicle and journey under retries.

Short stops must not split a journey. Immediate adapter power loss must not lose a
pending completion; the backend design must therefore include a bounded delayed
finalization mechanism rather than depending on another vehicle message.

## Energy semantics

Using consumption-positive vehicle power:

```text
energyDrawnKwh = integral(max(powerKw, 0), time)
energyRegenKwh = integral(max(-powerKw, 0), time)
energyNetKwh   = energyDrawnKwh - energyRegenKwh
netKwhPer100Km = energyNetKwh / distanceKm * 100
```

Existing one-minute signed-power History may support a first explicitly labelled
estimate. It must not be presented as a precise measurement until compared with
a higher-cadence or firmware-integrated reference. Long telemetry gaps are closed
at zero rather than integrated across.

The implemented backend consumes the already published
`bms/vehicle_power_w` signal, with `charging/power_signed` as fallback. It keeps
30-second power support points, ignores gaps longer than 90 seconds and writes a
single compact journey map into the existing per-vehicle session item. Repeated
SOC and movement observations are throttled. Emails produced by this path carry
the explicit flag `Energiequelle: Telemetrie-Schätzung`.

The parallel firmware contract is additive. REV11 on the N16 publishes
per-journey, non-decreasing counters on `journey/energy_drawn_wh`,
`journey/energy_regen_wh`, together with a stable
`journey/energy_counter_id`; the backend derives net energy from drawn minus
regenerated energy. When a complete, internally consistent counter set
is present, it automatically takes priority and the email carries
`Energiequelle: Firmware-Zähler`. Missing, reset or inconsistent firmware
counters fall back to the telemetry estimate; older devices remain compatible.

At an authoritative Standard-CAN charge boundary, the backend seals the journey
immediately but allows ten minutes for the final firmware checkpoint to arrive on
its separate MQTT topics. A complete and fresh counter ends that grace period
early and takes priority. If no usable firmware data arrives, the backend uses
the existing telemetry estimate after the bounded grace; legacy firmware needs
no new topic or migration.

For a normal stable `speed_zero` completion, an otherwise eligible journey now
allows a further ten minutes after the existing ten-minute stop confirmation for
a delayed firmware checkpoint. A complete fresh counter finalizes immediately
when it arrives. If it remains unavailable, finalization falls back to the
telemetry estimate after the bounded extension. Telemetry-timeout completions and
real eligibility exclusions are not delayed by this firmware-only wait.

The N16 implementation keeps its accumulator exclusively in RAM, integrates
fresh Standard-CAN vehicle power only while moving, rejects integration gaps
above two seconds and performs no counter-related NVS or filesystem writes. It
publishes three unretained values at journey start, every 60 seconds and at a
detected stop/charge boundary. Stops below ten minutes retain the same counter
identity; a later journey receives a new boot-nonce/sequence identity. A backend
freshness guard rejects a checkpoint older than the last observed movement, so
power loss before the final checkpoint selects the telemetry estimate instead of
an incomplete firmware value. XIAO compiles the shared stub with the counter
disabled.

The final REV11 builds use 58,600 bytes RAM and 1,377,732 bytes application flash
on N16. The N16 increment against the preceding measured build is approximately
112 bytes RAM and 10.1 KiB flash. XIAO remains at 57,860 bytes RAM; its binary is
1,421,040 of 1,703,936 bytes (83.40%) and passes the maintained 85% slot gate with
the counter disabled. Physical runtime heap, counter accuracy and one complete
road journey remain the controlled-pilot acceptance evidence.

## Portal preference contract

The existing notification preference gains `journeyEmailEnabled`, defaulting to
false. It is independently controlled from `enabled`, which remains the charging
target switch, but requires `emailEnabled` because both products reuse the same
confirmed SNS email channel. Older portal clients preserve the value while the
email channel remains enabled and clear it when that channel is disabled.

The hosted portal labels the setting as active for qualifying journeys and
states that the energy source is shown in the email. It is deployed with the
matching backend contract.

## Journey completion and cost boundary

Movement above 1 km/h starts or resumes a journey. A zero-speed observation marks
a normal completion candidate; resumed movement within ten minutes cancels it. A
five-minute EventBridge rule closes due candidates even when vehicle power has
disappeared, so delivery normally follows 10–15 minutes after the observed stop.
Completion is reserved once per user, vehicle and journey in the existing
expiring event table.

For the accepted Pioneer Standard-CAN decoder, `plugged=true` or
`is_charging=true` is an authoritative hard boundary. It seals and immediately
finalizes the preceding journey, retains an earlier zero-speed endpoint and
ignores later speed noise until that completion is persisted. Real movement after
completion starts a new journey. This replaces the historical Display-CAN-era
assumption that a charge indication might occur inside a drive.

After a 2026-08-26 road observation exposed a single false charge boundary at
83.5 km/h, the boundary gains a movement plausibility guard. Without an observed
speed-zero transition, plug/charge assertions are ignored while movement was
seen within the preceding two minutes. A real stationary charge continues
publishing and becomes authoritative after that bounded interval; a prior
speed-zero still permits immediate completion. Firmware with any configured
Standard-CAN V1/V2 input now also suppresses both Display-CAN `0x603` and `0x604`
for charging telemetry, rather than suppressing only the plug frame.

If coverage disappears before a terminal zero-speed or charging signal reaches
AWS, an independent inactivity path seals the journey after 30 minutes without
relevant speed, power, odometer or SOC telemetry. The endpoint is the timestamp
of the last relevant received signal; telemetry resuming before the timeout
cancels the offline marker. With the five-minute scheduler, finalization normally
occurs 30–35 minutes after that signal. The email explicitly labels this case as
`30-Min.-Telemetrie-Timeout` and states that its values end at the last received
signal, rather than implying coverage of unobserved garage distance.

No new telemetry publication is required for the estimate path. The existing
notification Lambda already receives the four-level MOT topics. Persisted power
support points are capped at one per 30 seconds and other repeated observations
are throttled, keeping storage to one compact session item per vehicle rather
than a sample archive.

The first physical road observation on 2026-08-14 exposed one completion edge:
the vehicle stopped after an otherwise qualifying roughly 10 km journey and was
plugged in about three minutes later. Charging during the ten-minute stability
window incorrectly marked the already stopped journey as charged and suppressed
the email. The state machine now records post-stop charging separately: it does
not disqualify a completed journey. The later hard Standard-CAN boundary
supersedes this intermediate resume behavior. Two focused regressions raised the
notification suite to 29 passing tests at that checkpoint. Change Set
`jny-001-post-stop-charge-20260815` deployed the correction in place; stack
`mot-dev-notifications` returned to `UPDATE_COMPLETE`, the Lambda is active and
successful, and its log group contains no new error. A second physical delivery
was subsequently received with plausible telemetry-estimate values.

On 2026-08-16 the first `xrpioneer2` road observation confirmed that the legacy
publisher supplied SOC, speed, odometer and signed-power History sufficient for
a telemetry estimate, but no journey event was created. Because the completed
state previously retained no decision reason, the exact rejection could not be
proven after finalization. Change Set `jny-001-exclusion-reason-20260816` therefore

`jny-001-charge-boundary-20260817` changes that signal into a hard journey

The maintainer confirmed receipt of the telemetry-estimate journey email with

## Acceptance gates

- preference defaults off and remains isolated by existing vehicle authorization;
- old preferences and old portal PUT requests remain compatible;
- portal cannot enable journey email without the email channel;
- no email is emitted by the preference-only slice;
- N16 flash/RAM/heap deltas are measured before firmware implementation proceeds;
- XIAO incompatibility does not create a fork or block N16;
- estimated and measured energy are labelled distinctly;
- retries, reconnects and short stops cannot duplicate or split a journey email;
- notification failure cannot affect telemetry ingestion, History or live state;
- local tests, controlled AWS deployment and physical journey validation pass
  before the pilot is called operational.
